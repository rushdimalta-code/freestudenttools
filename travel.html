When it's worth it: Almost always for your first arrival. You're jet-lagged, your phone may not have local data yet, and you have more luggage than fits in a standard cab. The premium over public transport is usually £15–30 — worth it once.

Flight Compensation

Can you claim if your flight was delayed or cancelled?

EU Regulation EC 261/2004 entitles passengers to €250–€600 if a flight from or into an EU airport is delayed 3+ hours or cancelled. Most passengers never claim — not because they're ineligible, but because they don't know they can. The claim window goes back 3–6 years in most countries.

What qualifies: Delays of 3+ hours, cancellations with less than 14 days' notice, denied boarding due to overbooking. Extraordinary circumstances (severe weather, ATC strikes) are usually excluded — mechanical faults are not.

Connectivity

Which eSIM works best for international students?

Roaming charges hit £10–20/day if you're unprepared. An eSIM activates on your phone instantly — no airport SIM queue, no physical card swap. Buy before you board and it starts working the moment you land. Works alongside your home SIM for calls and messages.

eSIM compatibility: iPhone XS (2018) and later, Google Pixel 3 (2018) and later, Samsung Galaxy S20 (2020) and later. Phone must be unlocked — not carrier-locked to your home network. Check: Settings → Mobile Data → Add eSIM.
Get a local SIM within 2–3 days. Your permanent local number — needed for bank accounts, uni admin, and landlords — should come from a local SIM. The eSIM bridges the gap until you can get to a phone shop.
