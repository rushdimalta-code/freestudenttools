Airport Transfers

How do you book a reliable airport transfer as an international student?

Arriving in a new country with heavy luggage and no local data is stressful enough. A pre-booked private transfer removes the guesswork — fixed price, driver waiting with your name, no negotiating at the rank.

🚗

GetTransfer — Pre-Book Airport Transfers in 1,000+ Cities

Search and book a private or shared transfer from the airport to your accommodation before you travel. Covers major student destinations: London, New York, Berlin, Sydney, Toronto, Singapore, and 1,000+ cities worldwide. Fixed price — no surge pricing, no surprises.

Book airport transfer → Affiliate disclosure: FreeStudentTools earns a commission if you book through this link, at no extra cost to you.

When is a pre-booked transfer worth it? Almost always for your first arrival. You're jet-lagged, your phone may not have local data yet, you don't know the city's taxi zones, and you have more luggage than fits in a standard cab. The premium over public transport is usually £15–30 — worth it once.

When to skip it: If your university has a free airport pick-up service (many do for international students around key arrival dates — check your international office website), use that instead.

Flight Compensation

Can you claim compensation if your flight is delayed or cancelled?

EU Regulation EC 261/2004 entitles passengers to €250–€600 if a flight departing from or arriving at an EU airport is delayed over 3 hours or cancelled without adequate notice. Most passengers never claim — not because they're ineligible, but because they don't know they can.

⚖️

AirHelp — Flight Delay & Cancellation Compensation

AirHelp checks your eligibility, files the claim, and handles the airline's legal team on your behalf. You pay nothing unless they win. Covers EU261/2004, UK261, and similar regulations in Canada, Brazil, and other jurisdictions. Up to €600 per passenger per flight.

Check my flight → Affiliate disclosure: FreeStudentTools earns a commission if compensation is awarded. No win, no fee — no charge if your claim is unsuccessful.

What qualifies? Delays of 3+ hours, cancellations with less than 14 days' notice, and denied boarding due to overbooking. Extraordinary circumstances (severe weather, air traffic control strikes) are usually excluded — mechanical faults and operational issues are not.

How far back can you claim? EU261 claims can go back up to 3 years in most EU countries, 6 years in the UK. If your flight last semester was delayed, it may still be claimable.

AirHelp's fee: They take a percentage of the compensation won (typically 25–35%) — you keep the rest. If they lose, you pay nothing.

Connectivity

Which eSIM works best for international students studying abroad?

Roaming charges hit £10–20/day if you're not prepared. An eSIM activates on your phone instantly — no queuing for a SIM at the airport, no swapping physical cards. Buy it before you board and it starts working the moment you land.

📱

Yesim — eSIM for 200+ Countries

Instant activation, 200+ countries covered, data plans from ~$4/day. Works on any unlocked iPhone (XS and later) or Android with eSIM support. Keep your home SIM active for calls — the eSIM runs alongside it. Perfect bridge until you get a local SIM card.

Get Yesim eSIM → Affiliate disclosure: FreeStudentTools earns a commission if you purchase, at no extra cost to you.

Does your phone support eSIM? iPhone XS (2018) and later, Google Pixel 3 (2018) and later, Samsung Galaxy S20 (2020) and later. Your phone must also be unlocked — not carrier-locked to your home network. Check in Settings → Mobile Data / Carrier.

Get a local SIM within 2–3 days. Your permanent local number — needed for bank accounts, university admin, and landlords — should come from a local SIM. The eSIM bridges the gap until you can get to a phone shop.
