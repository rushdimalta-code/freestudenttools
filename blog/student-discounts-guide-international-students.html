The Complete Student Discounts Guide for International Students 2026

📅 July 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 💰 150,000+ offers available 🌍 130+ countries covered

The average international student leaves £300–£500 on the table every year in unclaimed discounts. Not because the discounts don't exist — they're everywhere — but because nobody told them about the right cards, the right apps, and the right order to stack them.

Being a student in 2026 gives you access to a genuinely impressive set of discounts: half-price rail travel, free software that costs £600/year at retail, cheaper flights, and free museum entry in most European cities. FreeStudentTools has compiled the full picture — what actually saves the most, which cards are worth paying for, and what your university almost certainly provides for free that you haven't activated yet.

Is the ISIC Card Worth Buying?

Your university student card works within your campus and sometimes your city. The International Student Identity Card (ISIC) works in 130+ countries and is the globally recognised standard for proving student status to international retailers, airlines, and cultural institutions.

Key ISIC discounts in 2026 include:

  • Transport: Eurail student pricing (25% off), National Express UK (33% off coach), FlixBus (5–10% off), most major European national rail student rates, city transit day passes in most European capitals
  • Airlines: Turkish Airlines (discounts on selected routes), STA Travel student fares where available
  • Technology: Apple hardware discounts, Microsoft Office education pricing, Adobe Creative Cloud student pricing
  • Culture: Free or discounted entry to 10,000+ museums, galleries, and historic sites globally — including most national museums in the UK (which are free anyway) and most in Europe where a student ID reduces or eliminates entry fees
  • Streaming and subscriptions: Spotify student plan (50% off in most countries), Apple Music student plan, Amazon Prime Student
Buy ISIC in the cheapest country for it: The ISIC card costs $4 in some countries and $40 in others. If you're from a country where it's cheap, buy it before you travel. Check isic.org for your country's price.

Which UK Student Cards Are Worth Getting?

Totum Card (formerly NUS Extra)

If you're studying in the UK, Totum is worth considering. It costs £14.99/year for the basic tier or £49 for the 3-year Totum Pro. Discounts include ASOS (10%), Co-op (10% on food in some categories), Odeon and Vue cinemas (25% off), and hundreds of UK retailers.

The most valuable Totum benefit is access to the 16-25 Railcard — one-third off national rail fares across Great Britain. If you take even two long-distance rail journeys per year (London–Manchester return, for example), the railcard pays for itself. International students studying at UK institutions are eligible for Totum regardless of nationality.

16-25 Railcard (standalone)

If you're under 26 and studying in the UK, you can buy the 16-25 Railcard directly for £30/year (1-year) or £70 (3-year). One-third off all standard and first class rail fares. Off-peak restriction: minimum fare applies before 10am on weekdays. For anyone travelling between UK cities regularly, this is the single highest-value student discount available.

What Software Can Students Get for Free?

Before buying any software, check your university's IT portal. Most universities provide site licences that give students free access to tools that would otherwise cost hundreds of pounds per year:

  • Microsoft 365 Education — Free Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Teams, OneDrive (1TB). Available through most universities with a .edu or ac.uk email address.
  • Matlab — Free student licence at most engineering and science universities. The commercial version costs £800+/year.
  • SPSS Statistics — Free via IBM's student programme at many universities. The commercial licence is £1,200+/year.
  • Autodesk suite — AutoCAD, Fusion 360, Revit free for verified students. Architecture and engineering students: activate this immediately.
  • Adobe Creative Cloud — Some universities provide this at no cost via institutional licensing. Check before paying the student price (which is still 65% off the regular subscription).

Software you need to pay for — but at steep student discounts

  • Adobe Creative Cloud Student Plan — ~65% off the standard price. Still not free, but significantly cheaper than the professional rate.
  • Notion AI — Free for students with a verified academic email via Notion's Education Programme.
  • GitHub Pro — Free via GitHub Education (includes unlimited private repositories, GitHub Copilot, and developer tools).
  • Figma Education — Free professional tier for verified students. Includes everything on the paid plan.
  • Canva Pro — Free for verified students and educators via Canva for Education.
  • Spotify Student — 50% off in most countries; bundled with Hulu in the US.
  • Apple hardware — 10% discount with a valid student ID through Apple's Education Store. Larger discounts during back-to-school promotions (typically July–September).
GitHub Education Student Pack: This is genuinely underused. The GitHub Student Developer Pack bundles over $200,000 in free tools including free cloud credits (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), free domain names, and professional developer tools. Sign up at education.github.com with a university email.

Food and grocery discounts

Food costs are where student budgets take the biggest hit. Strategies that actually work:

  • UK: Co-op membership card gives 5% back on Co-op food purchases; some areas have student 10% deals with Totum. UNIDAYS and Student Beans aggregate hundreds of restaurant and food delivery discounts — always check before ordering Deliveroo or Uber Eats.
  • US: Many campus dining plans include meals in the cost of the meal plan. Off-campus: Student Beans and UNIDAYS have McDonald's, Chipotle, and restaurant chain deals. Amazon Prime Student (6-month free trial, then 50% off) includes Whole Foods discounts.
  • Germany/Netherlands: Student canteens (Mensa) are subsidised and extremely cheap — often €2–4 for a full meal. This is the most underused discount in continental Europe; non-German-speaking students sometimes don't realise the Mensa is open to all students.
  • Australia: Woolworths Everyday Rewards and Coles Flybuys loyalty programmes are free to join and accumulate points on every grocery shop. No specific student tier, but worth having from day one.

Travel discounts

Flights

StudentUniverse specialises in student fares for intercontinental travel and is worth checking against general booking sites. For European travel, budget airlines (Ryanair, Wizz Air, easyJet) rarely offer student discounts — their base prices are often already lower than discounted full-service fares. Eurail with ISIC pricing is often the best deal for multi-country European travel.

Buses and coaches

FlixBus (Europe and US) offers ISIC discounts. National Express (UK) is 33% off with ISIC or Totum. Megabus (UK/US) has no formal student discount but cheap advance fares that rival discounted alternatives.

Museums and culture

In the UK, most major national museums are free regardless of student status (Natural History Museum, V&A, British Museum, National Gallery). With a student card, many paid-entry attractions offer 20–30% off. In Europe, student discounts at museums are near-universal with any recognised student ID — ISIC works everywhere your local card doesn't.

The discount apps worth downloading

  • UNIDAYS — Free, huge directory of student discounts across 800+ brands. Works in UK, US, Australia, and 50+ countries. Always check here before any retail purchase.
  • Student Beans — Similar to UNIDAYS, some different brand partnerships. Worth having both — they don't overlap entirely.
  • Klook — Not exclusively student-focused, but heavily discounts activities, museum tickets, transport passes, and experiences in Asia, Europe, and Australia. Student rates on many listings.
  • Too Good To Go — App where restaurants and cafes sell unsold food at 1/3 of normal price at the end of the day. Available in 17+ countries. Popular with students in the UK, Germany, Netherlands, Denmark, France, and others. Not a "student discount" as such, but one of the best food hacks available.
The stack that saves the most: ISIC card + UNIDAYS app + Student Beans app + your university IT portal's free software + 16-25 Railcard (UK only). Set this up in your first week. The time investment is about 2 hours; the annual savings are typically £400–£800 depending on your spending patterns.

The bottom line

The discount that pays the most is almost always the software one — tools worth £600–£2,000 per year are genuinely free through your university or GitHub Education. After that, transport cards (particularly the 16-25 Railcard in the UK) are the highest-value paid cards for regular users. ISIC is worth having simply because it travels with you across 130 countries and works where local cards don't.

FreeStudentTools recommends spending one afternoon in your first week of university to activate everything: university IT portal, ISIC or Totum, UNIDAYS, Student Beans, GitHub Education, and whatever regional transit apps your city uses. You won't think about it again, and it'll quietly save you money all year.
