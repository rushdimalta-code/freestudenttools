Türkiye Bursları Scholarship 2026 — What Actually Gets You Selected

📅 July 2026 ⏱ 10 min read 🌍 180+ countries eligible 📅 40-day window: Jan–Feb

Türkiye Bursları opens for exactly 40 days each year — January 10 to February 20. Miss that window and you wait twelve months. Most international students don't know it exists until they miss the deadline. If you're reading this now, you're ahead of most applicants.

The scholarship is funded directly by the Turkish government through the Presidency for Turks Abroad and Related Communities (YTB). It's open to students from 180+ countries and covers everything: tuition, housing, stipend, flights, and health insurance. No partial funding. No means test. FreeStudentTools breaks down exactly what's covered, what the panel scores, and how to avoid the mistakes that sink most applications.

The full package includes:

  • Full tuition fees at your Turkish university — no cap on programme cost
  • Free dormitory accommodation at university housing throughout the programme
  • Monthly stipend: ₺13,500 for undergraduate, ₺18,000 for master's, ₺24,000 for PhD (2025 rates — revised annually with inflation)
  • Round-trip international airfare once per academic year
  • Comprehensive health insurance throughout the programme
  • Turkish language preparatory course — one full year, fully funded, if your degree is in Turkish
No prior university admission needed: Unlike most scholarships, you don't apply to a Turkish university first. You apply to Türkiye Bursları directly, list up to 12 programme preferences, and YTB handles placement. Acceptance from a university is not required at the application stage.

Who's eligible — and what trips people up

The headline eligibility is straightforward: citizens of 180+ countries, not Turkish. But the details matter:

  • Age cutoffs are firm: Under 21 for undergraduate programmes. Under 30 for master's. Under 35 for PhD. Under 50 for research scholarships. These are assessed at the scholarship start date, not the application date.
  • Academic minimums: 70% cumulative average for undergraduate, 75% for master's, 90% for health-related fields (medicine, dentistry, pharmacy). These are minimums — competitive applicants typically sit well above them.
  • Turkish citizenship history: If you've ever held Turkish citizenship or have Turkish ancestry as a primary identity, separate application pathways may apply. Check YTB's guidelines for your specific situation.
  • Current Turkish university students: If you're already enrolled at a Turkish university, you may not be eligible for Türkiye Bursları — check YTB's specific rules for transfer cases.
  • Previous Türkiye Bursları awardees: You cannot apply again at the same or lower degree level if you've previously received the scholarship.
The 12-preference strategy: You can select up to 12 university-programme combinations. The order matters — YTB treats your first preference as most desired. Many applicants rank prestigious Istanbul and Ankara universities first, even if those programmes are oversubscribed. If a less competitive but still excellent university is listed 8th, you may miss a placement you'd have loved.

What the selection panel actually evaluates

YTB doesn't publish a detailed scoring rubric the way some scholarships do, but based on the application requirements and patterns among successful applicants, selection weighs several factors:

Academic record

Your transcript is the primary filter. The minimum is a threshold — a 70% average gets you past the first screen but doesn't make you competitive. Applicants with 85%+ are in the competitive zone for most programmes. Health-related fields (medicine, dentistry) require 90% and have among the highest competition globally.

Study plan and motivation letter

This is where applications are won or lost at the shortlisting stage. A weak motivation letter describes what the scholarship covers and says you're grateful. A strong one answers three questions specifically: Why Turkey? — not "Turkey has a rich culture" but a specific academic or professional reason. Why this programme? — connecting your background to the curriculum. What will you do with this? — a concrete post-graduation plan that mentions your home country.

Language proficiency

English-taught programmes require IELTS or TOEFL scores. Turkish-taught programmes don't require Turkish upfront — the language year handles that. If you're applying to English programmes without a strong English score, that's a gap in your application.

Interview

Shortlisted candidates are called for an interview, either at Turkish embassies or online. Questions typically cover your study plan, your knowledge of Turkey and Turkish higher education, your post-graduation plans, and your ties to your home country. Interviewers look for students who can genuinely articulate why Turkey — not just "Turkey because I didn't get into the UK."

How to choose your 12 programmes wisely

This is the decision most applicants underinvest in. Your 12 preferences are ranked, and YTB uses them to place you. Here's what to consider:

  • Mix prestige levels: Include a few high-prestige universities (Boğaziçi, METU, Istanbul Technical), some mid-tier strong programmes, and a couple of solid regional universities. If you only list top-5 universities, you risk no placement at all.
  • Turkish vs English-taught: English-taught programmes require language scores and are more competitive among international applicants. Turkish-taught programmes have the language year built in — often less competition from non-Turkish-speaking countries.
  • Programme fit matters: YTB reviewers can tell when someone listed "Computer Engineering at all 12 universities" without thinking versus someone who chose programmes that align with a genuine academic direction.
  • Check accreditation: For health programmes especially, verify that the Turkish university's qualification will be recognised in your home country before you commit. Not all Turkish medical degrees are automatically recognised abroad.

How to write a motivation letter that survives shortlisting

YTB receives hundreds of thousands of applications. Motivation letters that get shortlisted are specific and honest. Here's the structure that works:

  1. Opening — the specific reason: Don't open with "I am honoured to apply for the prestigious Türkiye Bursları." Everyone writes that. Open with the specific reason you chose Turkey — a research collaboration, a professor whose work you've read, a language you want to learn, a career path that requires regional knowledge.
  2. Academic background — connect it: What have you studied? What did you achieve? How does it connect to what you're applying to study in Turkey? Be concrete.
  3. Why Turkey specifically: One sentence on geography or culture is fine. But what's the academic or professional case? Turkey's position at the intersection of Europe and Asia matters in certain fields — name yours.
  4. Post-graduation plan: YTB explicitly invests in students who will return home and contribute. Don't say "I will get a job." Say what role you want to play in your field in your country and how a Turkish degree enables it.
  5. Closing: One paragraph. Don't summarise everything you just wrote. Restate the connection between your goals and this specific programme.
Word count: YTB's portal limits the motivation statement. Stay within the character limit — applications that exceed it are truncated, not edited. Draft in a word processor first, then paste.

Document checklist

  • Transcript: Certified copy of your academic record with your cumulative GPA/average clearly shown. If your system doesn't show a percentage, provide conversion documentation.
  • Degree certificate: For graduate applicants, proof of your existing degree. If you haven't graduated yet, a statement of expected graduation may be accepted — check current YTB guidance.
  • Language score: IELTS or TOEFL for English programmes. No Turkish score needed for Turkish programmes.
  • Two photographs: Passport-style, recent. YTB specifies format requirements — follow them exactly.
  • Statement of intent / motivation letter
  • Recommendation letter: At least one academic recommendation. Two is better.
  • Passport copy
All documents must be in Turkish or English. Documents in other languages need certified translation. Self-translation is not accepted. Start the translation process before the application window opens — the 40-day window is too short to scramble for documents.

What happens after you're selected

Selected students receive a placement letter from YTB indicating which university and programme they've been assigned. If it's a Turkish-taught programme, you'll begin a one-year Turkish language preparatory course at a designated university before starting your degree.

The dormitory is provided through the university's student housing. Standards vary by university — Ankara and Istanbul universities tend to have well-maintained facilities, but it's a shared room, not a private apartment. Many students end up in private accommodation after year one if they find the dorm constraining.

The monthly stipend is paid in Turkish Lira. Given Turkey's recent inflation history, the stipend amount is revised annually — but costs have risen too. Major cities like Istanbul are significantly more expensive than Ankara or Bursa. Factor location into your preference list.

Alternatives if Türkiye Bursları doesn't work out

Some Turkish universities also offer separate institutional scholarships, including at Bilkent, Sabancı, and Koç — all private universities with strong research reputations. These are independent of Türkiye Bursları and some are partial rather than fully funded.

The bottom line

Türkiye Bursları is genuinely comprehensive — tuition, housing, stipend, flights, insurance, and a language year if you need it. The 40-day window is short and fixed. The motivation letter is the differentiator once you're past the academic screen.

Apply through the official TBBS portal at tbbs.turkiyeburslari.gov.tr. Start preparing your documents and motivation letter months before January — the students who rush the application in the 40-day window produce the weakest ones.
